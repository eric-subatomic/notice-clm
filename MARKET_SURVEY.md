# CLM market survey

Updated: 20 August 2026

## Executive view

Contract lifecycle management is not a single homogeneous category. It is at least six overlapping markets: enterprise-wide CLM, legal-led workflow, procurement and supplier contracting, revenue and sales contracting, lightweight contract management, and AI contract intelligence/review. Analyst definitions span the full lifecycle from initiation through negotiation, execution, compliance, and renewal. In practice, vendors converge on the same broad feature checklist while differing greatly in buyer, implementation burden, and the part of the lifecycle where they are strongest.

This survey maps 57 commercially relevant products and substitutes found in analyst coverage, review marketplaces, vendor comparisons, and current product sites. It is intentionally comprehensive rather than literally exhaustive: regional micro-vendors, bespoke consulting implementations, and products that only incidentally store contracts will continue to appear. The survey should be refreshed quarterly because acquisition and product consolidation are active in this market.

The main conclusion is that a new horizontal CLM cannot win by matching a generic feature checklist. The credible opening is a complete daily agreement-operations loop with a fast start, a calmer interaction model, and measurable reductions in drafting time, approval delay, and missed obligations.

### 7. Do-it-yourself substitutes

The most important competitors are often not CLM vendors at all: SharePoint plus Power Automate, Google Drive plus Sheets, Airtable, Notion, Monday.com, Odoo, ERPNext, and a shared inbox. These win through familiarity, perceived low cost, and flexibility. Their recurring failures are inconsistent metadata, dependence on one administrator, manual reminders, fragmented ownership, and weak auditability.

## Capability convergence

Nearly every full CLM now claims some combination of:

- intake forms and request routing;
- templates, clause libraries, document generation and Word integration;
- collaborative review, redlining and version control;
- approvals and configurable workflow;
- e-signature;
- central repository, access control and audit history;
- AI extraction, summaries, Q&A, playbook review and suggested redlines;
- obligation, milestone, expiry and renewal tracking;
- dashboards, reporting and integrations.

That convergence makes “all-in-one CLM with AI” an undifferentiated new-product thesis. The durable differentiation is distribution, installed-system integration, data model depth, implementation capability, a vertical workflow, or ownership of a measurable business outcome.

## Unmet need selected for the initial product

The chosen opening is **general-purpose agreement operations for lean legal and business teams**.

The market already offers feature-rich horizontal CLM, but implementation-heavy configuration and dense administrative interfaces leave daily participants working in email, Word, e-signature, CRM attachments, tickets, and shared drives. Notice competes across the lifecycle while entering through the operational experience: one visible queue and one document-centered agreement room from intake or import through negotiation, approval, signature, and active obligations.

The scope is broad enough to replace the daily handoffs while the product model stays opinionated:

1. Choose a sell-side, buy-side, or corporate lane and start from an exact published template version, a version-pinned intake form, business data, or an imported file.
2. Confirm parties and related entities, dates, per-currency minor-unit value, renewal, payment, governing law, typed custom fields, and risk flags.
3. Review material changes, consult customer-approved language, and search extractor-registered fragments pinned to an immutable document version/hash and caller-supplied source-text digest. Reviewers must verify citations against the protected working paper; cited extraction proposals carry confidence and human review evidence but never apply themselves or claim a legal/playbook verdict.
4. Route contextual approvals through a published workflow snapshot and direct/group lane authority with exact currency and optional amount scope.
5. Send a governed DocuSeal 3.2.1 packet through provider email delivery and retain verified execution evidence; evaluation signing is an entirely local simulation that neither embeds nor contacts DocuSeal.
6. Operate renewals, milestones, and obligations from the same searchable record, bounded live work-queue pagination, saved views, and per-currency portfolio reporting.

Why this operating-model wedge is the strongest initial path:

| Candidate wedge | Urgency | Frequency | Buyer clarity | Initial-scope simplicity | Competitive crowding | Overall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AI redlining for sales contracts | 4 | 5 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 16/25 |
| General repository and AI search | 3 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 1 | 15/25 |
| Legal request intake | 4 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 18/25 |
| Obligation compliance for regulated enterprise | 5 | 4 | 4 | 1 | 3 | 17/25 |
| Opinionated agreement operations | 5 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | **22/25** |

The economic story combines faster revenue activation, controlled spend, shorter legal queues, and fewer missed commitments. The product does not attempt to out-configure enterprise CLM; it wins through a complete default lifecycle, less workflow ceremony, context-rich queues, governed intake and reusable paper, version/hash-pinned registered citation evidence with required human source verification, a document-centered agreement room, and deployment-selected OCR through a signed customer-hosted scanner gateway. A business-friendly no-code designer beyond the governed JSON contracts, broader extraction/CDR and proposal-application providers beyond the shipped OCR path, bulk migration execution beyond bounded ingestion inventory, additional large-estate analytics, and additional identity/provider adapters remain expansion layers.

## Sources and method

The survey triangulated current product pages with [Gartner’s 2025 CLM market coverage](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7159730), [Forrester’s 2025 Wave](https://www.forrester.com/report/the-forrester-wave-tm-contract-lifecycle-management-platforms-q1-2025/RES181997), the [G2 CLM category](https://www.g2.com/categories/contract-lifecycle-management-clm), and current market commentary. G2 notes that implementation commonly takes three to six months, while vendors such as SpotDraft now compete on going live in weeks. Juro’s market guide describes the status quo as contracts moving across Word, email, e-signature, shared drives, and spreadsheets. These patterns, plus practitioner reports of expensive platforms and broken data-entry workflows, informed the selected wedge.

Vendor claims in this document describe positioning, not independently verified product performance. Pricing, packaging, ownership, and feature availability should be confirmed during procurement.
